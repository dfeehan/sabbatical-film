# Reading Companion

**Essays to read *after* each film — 24 Aug 2026 – 4 Jul 2027**

Organized by unit, week, and film, matching [the syllabus](sabbatical-film-syllabus.md). Optional-extension films are marked *(optional)*.

**The rule from the syllabus still holds: watch first, write your three or four sentences, *then* read.** These are here so that when you want a second opinion you have one within a click, not so that you arrive at the film pre-interpreted.

**About the sources.** Criterion's Current essays are free and are usually the best single starting point for any film in their collection. Roger Ebert's *Great Movies* essays are free, short, and unpretentious. Senses of Cinema is free, longer, and more academic — its Great Directors entries are the fastest way to place a film in a career. David Bordwell's *Observations on Film Art* is the formalist option and the one closest to the vocabulary in *Film Art*; where he's written on a film it's usually the most technically illuminating thing available. Scraps from the Loft reprints out-of-print criticism (Kael, Sarris, Kauffmann) that is otherwise hard to find legally online. Items marked *(paywalled)* need a subscription; a few need library access.

**A note on links.** Every URL here was loaded and checked against the essay it claims to be, as of August 2026. If one eventually 404s, searching the essay title plus the site name will usually find it — Criterion occasionally renumbers.

## Unit 1 · The Grammar

*[← this unit in the syllabus](sabbatical-film-syllabus.md#unit-1--the-grammar)*

### Week 1 · Aug 24–30

**Citizen Kane** (1941, Welles)

- [Citizen Kane: The Once and Future Kane](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/7613-citizen-kane-the-once-and-future-kane) — Criterion. The film's critical fortunes, from Sarris vs. Kael onward.
- [Citizen Kane](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-citizen-kane-1941) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. The standard appreciation of deep focus and "emotional chronology."
- [Welles at 101, KANE at 75 or thereabouts](https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2016/05/06/welles-at-101-kane-at-75-or-thereabouts/) — David Bordwell. What Welles actually invented versus what he inherited — the corrective to the origin myth.

**Rear Window** (1954, Hitchcock)

- [Rear Window](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-rear-window-1954) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Treats the film as a feature-length demonstration of montage and complicity.
- [Recuperation and Rear Window](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2003/feature-articles/rear_window/) — Senses of Cinema. The courtyard read through format and the architecture of looking.
- [Everything Jeff saw in "Rear Window"](https://www.rogerebert.com/features/everything-jeff-saw-in-rear-window) — RogerEbert.com. A visual inventory of the apartment-block "screens."

**North by Northwest** (1959, Hitchcock) *(optional)*

- [North by Northwest: Long shots as close-ups](https://www.rogerebert.com/scanners/north-by-northwest-long-shots-as-close-ups-part-1) — Jim Emerson. Close formal analysis of Hitchcock's scaling and vectors — the crop-duster sequence taken apart.
- [North by Northwest archive review](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/north-northwest-archive-review-pure-entertainment-that-never-puts-foot-wrong) — *Sight & Sound*. The contemporary verdict, from the archive.

### Week 2 · Aug 31–Sep 6

**Do the Right Thing** (1989, Lee)

- [Do the Right Thing](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/98-do-the-right-thing) — Criterion. The Cannes premiere and the film's refusal of easy sides.
- [Do the Right Thing: Walking in Stereo](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/6506-do-the-right-thing-walking-in-stereo) — Criterion. Radio Raheem, sound, and the block as acoustic space.
- [Right hand, left hand: "Do the Right Thing" at 25](https://www.rogerebert.com/mzs/right-hand-left-hand-do-the-right-thing-at-25) — Matt Zoller Seitz. Formal reappraisal at the 25-year mark.

**Chungking Express** (1994, Wong Kar-wai)

- [Chungking Express: Electric Youth](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/766-chungking-express-electric-youth) — Criterion. Frames the film as the *Masculin féminin* of the nineties.
- [Narrative Symmetry in Chungking Express](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/5498-on-the-channel-narrative-symmetry-in-chungking-express) — Criterion. Short video-essay piece on the two-halves structure.
- [Wong Kar-wai](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/wong/) — Senses of Cinema, Great Directors. Career overview, strong on the step-printed editing vocabulary.

**Killer of Sheep** (1978, Burnett) *(optional)*

- [Killer of Sheep: Everyday Blues](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8817-killer-of-sheep-everyday-blues) — Criterion. Watts, Black American music, and the texture of dailiness.
- [Frontality and Time](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8780-frontality-and-time) — Criterion. Formal essay on Burnett's staging and duration.
- [Extraordinary ordinary people](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-killer-of-sheep-1977) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Ebert correcting his own 1977 review; plotlessness as statement.

### Week 3 · Sep 7–13

**Rashomon** (1950, Kurosawa)

- [The Rashomon Effect](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/195-the-rashomon-effect) — Criterion. The four-testimony structure and its long afterlife.
- [Rashomon Rhapsody](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/1202-rashomon-rhapsody) — Michael Sragow, Criterion. Tests whether the film survives decades of imitation.
- [Rashomon](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-rashomon-1950) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Insists there's an explanation but no solution.

**The 400 Blows** (1959, Truffaut)

- [The 400 Blows: Close to Home](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/528-the-400-blows-close-to-home) — Criterion. The film as record and exorcism of Truffaut's own childhood.
- [The 400 Blows](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-400-blows-1959) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Especially good on the final freeze-frame.
- [Truffaut's The 400 Blows, or the Sea, Antoine, the Sea…](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2000/francois-truffaut/blows/) — Senses of Cinema. Situates it among Truffaut's films about childhood.

**A Man Escaped** (1956, Bresson) *(optional)*

- [A Man Escaped: Quintessential Bresson](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2628-a-man-escaped-quintessential-bresson) — Criterion. Bresson's method at its most distilled.
- [David Bordwell and Kristin Thompson Listen to A Man Escaped](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2705-david-bordwell-and-kristin-thompson-listen-to-a-man-escaped) — Bordwell & Thompson, Criterion. Visual essay on Bresson's nonsimultaneous sound.
- [A Man Escaped](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2012/cteq/a-man-escaped/) — Gwendolyn Audrey Foster, Senses of Cinema. Economy, off-screen violence, free will.

## Unit 2 · The Studio System

*[← this unit in the syllabus](sabbatical-film-syllabus.md#unit-2--the-studio-system)*

### Week 4 · Sep 14–20 — Screwball

**It Happened One Night** (1934, Capra)

- [It Happened One Night: Screwball Comedy?](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3372-it-happened-one-night-screwball-comedy) — Molly Haskell & Phillip Lopate, Criterion. Two critics arguing over whether the genre existed yet.
- [It Happened One Night: All Aboard!](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3369-it-happened-one-night-all-aboard) — Criterion. How the film became a how-to manual for imitators.
- [It Happened One Night](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2001/cteq/happened/) — Senses of Cinema. Good on the class politics under the romance.

**His Girl Friday** (1940, Hawks)

- [David Bordwell on the Magic of His Girl Friday](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/4395-david-bordwell-on-the-magic-of-his-girl-friday) — Bordwell, Criterion. Framing, staging, and cutting choices you'd otherwise miss. **Start here.**
- [Words in Edgewise: Dialogue in His Girl Friday](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/4388-words-in-edgewise-dialogue-in-his-girl-friday) — Criterion. How 191 script pages fit into 92 minutes.
- [His Girl Friday: The Perfect Remarriage](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/4380-his-girl-friday-the-perfect-remarriage) — Criterion. The most grown-up of the remarriage comedies.
- [Opening Shots: His Girl Friday](https://www.rogerebert.com/scanners/opening-shots-his-girl-friday) — Jim Emerson. Unpacks the newsroom tracking shot.

**The Lady Eve** (1941, Sturges) *(optional)*

- [The Lady Eve: Sweet Revenge](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/7021-the-lady-eve-sweet-revenge) — Criterion. Sturges at the top of his run.
- [The Lady Eve](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/135-the-lady-eve) — James Harvey, Criterion. On the film's "energetic cruelty" and Stanwyck's command.
- [The Lady Eve](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-lady-eve-1941) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*.

### Week 5 · Sep 21–27 — The Western

**Red River** (1948, Hawks)

- [Red River: The Longest Drive](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3176-red-river-the-longest-drive) — Criterion. The hinge of Hawks's career and his Cahiers rediscovery.
- [Hooked on Film: David Thomson on Howard Hawks's Red River](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/3922-hooked-on-film-david-thomson-on-howard-hawks-s-red-river) — David Thomson, Criterion. A critic's formative encounter.
- [Red River](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-red-river-1948) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. On the older-man/younger-man core, and where the film wobbles.

**The Searchers** (1956, Ford)

- [The Searchers](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-searchers-1956) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Confronts head-on whether the film endorses or indicts Ethan's racism.
- Pair with the unit's assigned reading — Manny Farber's "Underground Films" (1957) — which is largely an argument *against* the prestige-Ford register on display here.

**Stagecoach** (1939, Ford) *(optional)*

- [Stagecoach: Taking the Stage](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/1472-stagecoach-taking-the-stage) — Criterion. How Ford bought the story himself and turned a B-picture into myth.

### Week 6 · Sep 28–Oct 4 — Noir

**Double Indemnity** (1944, Wilder)

- [Double Indemnity](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-double-indemnity-1944) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. On Seitz's photography and the "enigma that keeps it new."
- [Billy Wilder, Movie-Maker: Critical Essays on the Films](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2011/book-reviews/billy-wilder-movie-maker-critical-essays-on-the-films-edited-by-karen-mcnally/) — Senses of Cinema. Book review whose centerpiece is on masking and ethics in this film.

**The Third Man** (1949, Reed)

- [The Third Man](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/25-the-third-man) — Michael Wilmington, Criterion. Moral corruption rendered as visual complexity.
- [Listening to The Third Man](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/482-listening-to-the-third-man) — Criterion. Anton Karas's zither and what the score does.
- [The Third Man](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-third-man-1949) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Vienna's rubble, canted angles, postwar knowledge.

**Out of the Past** (1947, Tourneur) *(optional)*

- [Far from Home: Three Noirs by Jacques Tourneur](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/9137-far-from-home-three-noirs-by-jacques-tourneur) — Criterion. Tourneur's noirs as stories of displacement.

### Week 7 · Oct 5–11 — Hollywood on itself

**Singin' in the Rain** (1952, Donen & Kelly)

- [Singin' in the Rain](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-singin-in-the-rain-1952) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Why the film's freshness hasn't dated.
- [La La Land: Singin' in the Sun](https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2017/02/11/la-la-land-singin-in-the-sun/) — Bordwell. Uses *Singin'* as the yardstick for what a movie musical actually does.

**Sunset Boulevard** (1950, Wilder)

- [Sunset Boulevard](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-sunset-boulevard-1950) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Especially strong on von Stroheim as the film's emotional anchor.
- [Sunset Blvd.](https://www.deepfocusreview.com/definitives/sunset-blvd/) — Deep Focus Review. Extended reading of Hollywood's greatest self-satire.

**The Bad and the Beautiful** (1952, Minnelli) *(optional)*

- No strong free essay found. Senses of Cinema's Minnelli entry is the fallback.

## Unit 3 · Silents, the Short Version

### Week 8 · Oct 12–18 — Keaton

**Sherlock Jr.** (1924, Keaton)

- [Sherlock Jr. (1924)](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-film-preservation-board/documents/Sherlock-Jr_Vance.pdf) — Jeffrey Vance, Library of Congress (PDF). The best short scholarly account of the production and the gags.
- [Directors: Keaton](https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/category/directors-keaton/) — Bordwell. His collected entries on Keaton's geometry and gag construction — the syllabus's assigned reading for this unit.
- [The Films of Buster Keaton](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-films-of-buster-keaton) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Overview essay with *Sherlock Jr.* at its center.

**The General** (1926, Keaton)

- [The General](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-general-1927) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Argues Keaton has aged better than Chaplin, and why.
- [The General (1926)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/136a6211-90d9-5d18-b047-7967deac8bab/the-general) — BFI. Concise account of the flop-to-canon reversal.

**City Lights** (1931, Chaplin) *(optional)*

- [City Lights: The Immortal Tramp](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2957-city-lights-the-immortal-tramp) — Criterion. Chaplin's fusion of burlesque and pathos.
- [Shooting City Lights](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2959-shooting-city-lights) — Criterion. The famously agonized production, including the flower-girl meeting.
- [City Lights](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-city-lights-1931) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*.

### Week 9 · Oct 19–25 — Expressionism

**The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari** (1920, Wiene)

- [A world slanted at sharp angles](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-cabinet-of-dr-caligari-1920) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. The first true horror film, and its break from photographic realism.
- [100 years of The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari: why we're still living in its shadows](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/100-years-cabinet-dr-caligari/) — BFI. Centenary essay on the long influence — including on Week 6's noir.

**Sunrise** (1927, Murnau)

- [Sunrise](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-sunrise-1928) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. On the camera finally learning to fly.
- [Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2022/cteq/sunrise-a-song-of-two-humans-f-w-murnau-1927/) — Senses of Cinema. Murnau's first American film and its formal ambitions.

**The Passion of Joan of Arc** (1928, Dreyer)

- [The Bells of Saint Joan](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/5486-the-bells-of-saint-joan) — Criterion. Close-ups, oblique angles, and the trial as a spatial problem.
- [The Passion of Joan of Arc](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-the-passion-of-joan-of-arc-1928) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Centered on Falconetti's face.

**Metropolis** (1927, Lang) *(optional)*

- [Metropolis](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-metropolis-1927) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. The film that fixed the image of the future city.
- [Lang, Fritz](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/lang/) — Senses of Cinema, Great Directors.

**Nosferatu** (1922, Murnau) *(optional)*

- [Nosferatu](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-nosferatu-1922) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. The vampire film before the clichés set.
- [Nosferatu: Eine Symphonie des Grauens](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2022/cteq/nosferatu-eine-symphonie-des-grauens-nosferatu-a-symphony-of-horror-f-w-murnau-1922/) — Senses of Cinema. Murnau embodying rather than explaining the myth.

### Week 10 · Oct 26–Nov 1 — Montage and the avant-garde

**Man with a Movie Camera** (1929, Vertov)

- [A film about a film about itself](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/great-movie-man-with-a-movie-camera-1929) — Roger Ebert, *Great Movies*. Includes a striking average-shot-length comparison.
- [Man with a Movie Camera (1929)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/cacbb2df-be18-5e59-b5c3-f0eb9ea3a90d/man-with-a-movie-camera) — BFI. Short but sharp on Vertov's rejection of intertitles and actors.

**Battleship Potemkin** (1925, Eisenstein)

- [Directors: Eisenstein](https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/category/directors-eisenstein/) — Bordwell. Collected entries on Eisenstein and montage; the clearest gloss on the theory the syllabus asks you to skim.
- [Battleship Potemkin](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2000/cteq/potemkin/) — Senses of Cinema. The Odessa Steps sequence and the myth-making cycle it belongs to.
- [1925: Battleship Potemkin](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2017/soviet-cinema/1925-battleship-potemkin-sergei-eisenstein/) — Senses of Cinema. The film's actual (small) initial audience versus its legend.

**Un Chien Andalou** (1929, Buñuel & Dalí)

- [Un Chien Andalou](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2001/cteq/chien/) — Senses of Cinema. Buñuel's obsessions and his stated hostility to the "avant-garde."
- [Luis Buñuel: Eternal Surrealist](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/6767-luis-bunuel-eternal-surrealist) — Criterion. Career overview beginning with the shock films.

**Meshes of the Afternoon** (1943, Deren)

- [Deren, Maya](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/deren-2/) — Senses of Cinema, Great Directors. On the doubling, tripling, and cyclic structure.
- [Teaching Meshes of the Afternoon](https://www.thecine-files.com/teaching-meshes-of-the-afternoon/) — The Cine-Files. A dossier of scholars on how the film is read and taught — unusually good if you want to see the disagreements laid out.

**The Crowd** (1928, Vidor) *(optional)*

- [The Crowd](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2013/cteq/the-crowd/) — Raymond Durgnat & Scott Simmon, Senses of Cinema. Locates the film in an international wave of screen populism.
- [The Man and The Crowd (1928): Photography, Film, and Fate](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/the-man-and-the-crowd/) — The Public Domain Review. On the film's urban imagery and its literary ancestors.
