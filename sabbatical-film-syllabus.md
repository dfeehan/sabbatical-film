# A Sabbatical in Film

**42 weeks · 24 Aug 2026 – 4 Jul 2027 · 85 core films, ~127 with extensions**

---

## How to use this

Each week has **two core films** and **one optional extension**. Two is the floor; take the extension in weeks when you have the time or the unit has its hooks in you. That lands you at 2–3 films a week as planned, without the schedule becoming a debt you owe.

Four weeks list three or four core films rather than two. In every case the extras are **short** — 14 to 32 minutes — and are marked as such. They're there because avant-garde and documentary work is where the formal vocabulary gets isolated most clearly, and skipping it entirely would leave a real hole. A 28-minute film is not a third film; it's an appendix to the one before it.

Films are grouped into **13 units of 3–4 weeks**. Within a unit, the films talk to each other — an influence, a rebuttal, a second attempt at the same problem. This is the whole design. A single Antonioni film is a slog; three Antonioni-adjacent films in a row and you start to see what he was actually doing, which is when it gets interesting.

**Three rules that matter more than the list:**

1. **One film per sitting, no phone.** These were built for uninterrupted attention and collapse without it.
2. **Write 3–4 sentences after each one** — before you read anything about it. What did the camera do? What surprised you? What annoyed you? You know better than most that unrecorded observation evaporates.
3. **Read the criticism *after*, never before.** One piece, ten minutes. Unit-level reading is listed below; the [Reading Companion](reading-companion.md) has linked essays for every individual film.

**Runtimes** are listed after the director. They're for the standard version — the Criterion release where one exists, otherwise the most widely circulating restoration. Silents and older restorations vary by a few minutes depending on projection speed and transfer; where a film exists in genuinely different cuts (*Apocalypse Now*, *Andrei Rublev*, *Metropolis*), the entry says which one is meant.

**Where to find these:** the Criterion Channel covers a large majority of this list and is the single best subscription for the project. MUBI, Kanopy (free with most university logins — check Berkeley's), and your library fill most of the rest.

**Optional scaffolding:** Bordwell & Thompson, *Film Art: An Introduction*. Read chapters 4–6 (mise-en-scène, cinematography, editing) during Unit 1. It's about 100 pages and it gives you vocabulary you'll use for the remaining 40 weeks. This is the closest thing to the class you never took.

**If you fall behind:** don't catch up. Drop the extensions, drop a week, rejoin at the next unit boundary. The units are self-contained on purpose.

## Unit 1 · The Grammar
### Weeks 1–3 · 24 Aug – 13 Sep 2026

Six films chosen because each one makes a formal technique impossible to miss. Watch these with Bordwell open. The goal isn't to love them — it's to leave with words for what you're seeing, so that everything after lands differently.

**Week 1 · Aug 24–30**

- [*Citizen Kane*](reading-companion.md#citizen-kane-1941-welles) (1941, Welles · 119 min) — deep focus, and a film that narrates itself in fragments. The whole vocabulary of "showy" filmmaking starts here.
- [*Rear Window*](reading-companion.md#rear-window-1954-hitchcock) (1954, Hitchcock · 112 min) — the purest demonstration of how editing constructs meaning. Two shots, one implication, no dialogue.
- *Optional:* [*North by Northwest*](reading-companion.md#north-by-northwest-1959-hitchcock-optional) (1959, Hitchcock · 136 min) — the crop-duster sequence is the standard textbook example of how editing builds tension out of nothing. Hitchcock appears in these academic syllabi more than any other director, and this is the film they teach.

**Week 2 · Aug 31 – Sep 6**

- [*Do the Right Thing*](reading-companion.md#do-the-right-thing-1989-lee) (1989, Lee · 120 min) — color, heat, and camera height as argument. Notice the Dutch angles arrive before the violence does.
- [*Chungking Express*](reading-companion.md#chungking-express-1994-wong-kar-wai) (1994, Wong Kar-wai · 102 min) — step-printing, handheld, and the discovery that a film's *texture* can carry its emotion entirely.
- *Optional:* [*Killer of Sheep*](reading-companion.md#killer-of-sheep-1978-burnett-optional) (1978, Burnett · 80 min)

**Week 3 · Sep 7–13**

- [*Rashomon*](reading-companion.md#rashomon-1950-kurosawa) (1950, Kurosawa · 88 min) — the same events, four times. Watch how the camera's behavior changes with each teller.
- [*The 400 Blows*](reading-companion.md#the-400-blows-1959-truffaut) (1959, Truffaut · 99 min) — the long take and the freeze frame; how withholding a cut creates unease.
- *Optional:* *Bicycle Thieves* is coming in Unit 4 — instead try [*A Man Escaped*](reading-companion.md#a-man-escaped-1956-bresson-optional) (1956, Bresson · 101 min)

**Read:** Roger Ebert's *Great Movies* essay on *Citizen Kane*; Bordwell & Thompson chs. 4–6.

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-1--the-grammar) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 2 · The Studio System
### Weeks 4–7 · 14 Sep – 11 Oct 2026

Classical Hollywood at full operating efficiency. These are, without qualification, fun — which is the point. Genre is a set of constraints, and watching great directors work inside constraints is the fastest way to see directorial choice at all. Pay attention to how much these films accomplish in 95 minutes.

**Week 4 · Sep 14–20 — Screwball**

- [*It Happened One Night*](reading-companion.md#it-happened-one-night-1934-capra) (1934, Capra · 105 min)
- [*His Girl Friday*](reading-companion.md#his-girl-friday-1940-hawks) (1940, Hawks · 92 min) — the dialogue overlaps by design. Don't fight it.
- *Optional:* [*The Lady Eve*](reading-companion.md#the-lady-eve-1941-sturges-optional) (1941, Sturges · 94 min)

**Week 5 · Sep 21–27 — The Western**

- [*Red River*](reading-companion.md#red-river-1948-hawks) (1948, Hawks · 127 min)
- [*The Searchers*](reading-companion.md#the-searchers-1956-ford) (1956, Ford · 119 min) — the most influential and most morally uncomfortable American film of its decade. The doorway shots bookend it for a reason.
- *Optional:* [*Stagecoach*](reading-companion.md#stagecoach-1939-ford-optional) (1939, Ford · 96 min)

**Week 6 · Sep 28 – Oct 4 — Noir**

- [*Double Indemnity*](reading-companion.md#double-indemnity-1944-wilder) (1944, Wilder · 107 min)
- [*The Third Man*](reading-companion.md#the-third-man-1949-reed) (1949, Reed · 104 min) — noir style exported to bombed-out Vienna, with a zither.
- *Optional:* [*Out of the Past*](reading-companion.md#out-of-the-past-1947-tourneur-optional) (1947, Tourneur · 97 min)

**Week 7 · Oct 5–11 — Hollywood on itself**

- [*Singin' in the Rain*](reading-companion.md#singin-in-the-rain-1952-donen--kelly) (1952, Donen & Kelly · 103 min)
- [*Sunset Boulevard*](reading-companion.md#sunset-boulevard-1950-wilder) (1950, Wilder · 110 min) — the same subject, the sound-era transition, told as a horror film. Watch these back to back and the pairing does the work.
- *Optional:* [*The Bad and the Beautiful*](reading-companion.md#the-bad-and-the-beautiful-1952-minnelli-optional) (1952, Minnelli · 118 min)

**Read:** Manny Farber, "Underground Films" (1957) — his case for Hawks, Walsh, and Mann as unpretentious craftsmen, and against exactly the prestige-Ford register of *The Searchers*. Useful precisely because it argues with this week's assignments.

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-2--the-studio-system) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 3 · Silents, the Short Version
### Weeks 8–10 · 12 Oct – 1 Nov 2026

You said silents are less appealing, so this unit is deliberately small and front-loaded with pleasure. Note the runtimes — **every core film in this unit is under 95 minutes**, and four are under 70. Keaton first, because *Sherlock Jr.* is 45 minutes of the most inventive physical filmmaking ever committed, and it will recalibrate your expectations before you meet Dreyer.

Weeks 9 and 10 carry an extra title each. Week 9 adds German Expressionism, which the plan otherwise skipped entirely and which every film-history course treats as foundational — and which Unit 2's noir week is directly descended from. Week 10 adds two very short avant-garde films for the same reason: they isolate what narrative films do invisibly.

**Week 8 · Oct 12–18 — Keaton**

- [*Sherlock Jr.*](reading-companion.md#sherlock-jr-1924-keaton) (1924, Keaton · 45 min) — a projectionist walks into the movie he's showing. In 1924.
- [*The General*](reading-companion.md#the-general-1926-keaton) (1926, Keaton · 79 min)
- *Optional:* [*City Lights*](reading-companion.md#city-lights-1931-chaplin-optional) (1931, Chaplin · 87 min)

**Week 9 · Oct 19–25 — Expressionism and the image at full power**

- [*The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari*](reading-companion.md#the-cabinet-of-dr-caligari-1920-wiene) (1920, Wiene · 77 min) — painted shadows, tilted walls, a set that behaves like a state of mind. Watch it and Week 6's noir stops looking like an American invention.
- [*Sunrise*](reading-companion.md#sunrise-1927-murnau) (1927, Murnau · 94 min) — Murnau brings the Expressionist toolkit to Hollywood; the camera moves like nothing else would for thirty years.
- [*The Passion of Joan of Arc*](reading-companion.md#the-passion-of-joan-of-arc-1928-dreyer) (1928, Dreyer · 82 min) — almost entirely faces. It is not a comfortable film, but it is a short one.
- *Optional:* [*Metropolis*](reading-companion.md#metropolis-1927-lang-optional) (1927, Lang · 148 min) or [*Nosferatu*](reading-companion.md#nosferatu-1922-murnau-optional) (1922, Murnau · 94 min)

**Week 10 · Oct 26 – Nov 1 — Montage and the avant-garde**

- [*Man with a Movie Camera*](reading-companion.md#man-with-a-movie-camera-1929-vertov) (1929, Vertov · 68 min) — a city symphony and a film about filmmaking; no plot, no intertitles, endlessly watchable.
- [*Battleship Potemkin*](reading-companion.md#battleship-potemkin-1925-eisenstein) (1925, Eisenstein · 75 min) — read the Odessa Steps sequence as the argument for editing that everything after is answering.
- [*Un Chien Andalou*](reading-companion.md#un-chien-andalou-1929-buñuel--dalí) (1929, Buñuel & Dalí · 16 min) — Surrealism's demonstration that film can dispense with continuity entirely and still hold you.
- [*Meshes of the Afternoon*](reading-companion.md#meshes-of-the-afternoon-1943-deren) (1943, Deren · 14 min) — the most-taught avant-garde film there is; it appears in more of the academic syllabi than *His Girl Friday*. Watch it twice — it's fourteen minutes.
- *Optional:* [*The Crowd*](reading-companion.md#the-crowd-1928-vidor-optional) (1928, Vidor · 98 min)

*Four films, but the last two total half an hour. Real week runtime: about 2h 50m.*

**Read:** David Bordwell's blog, "Sherlock Jr." posts; Eisenstein's short essay "A Dialectic Approach to Film Form" (skim — the theory is dense, the examples are clear).

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-3--silents-the-short-version) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 4 · Italy: Rubble to Ennui
### Weeks 11–13 · 2 – 22 Nov 2026

Postwar Italian cinema runs an arc in fifteen years from filming actual bombed streets with non-actors to filming rich people being bored in beautiful rooms. That arc is one of the most legible in film history, and each film comments on the last.

**Week 11 · Nov 2–8**

- [*Rome, Open City*](reading-companion.md#rome-open-city-1945-rossellini) (1945, Rossellini · 103 min) — shot in the months right after the Germans left Rome, partly on scrounged film stock, in a city that hadn't yet been repaired.
- [*Bicycle Thieves*](reading-companion.md#bicycle-thieves-1948-de-sica) (1948, De Sica · 89 min) — the neorealist thesis in its clearest form: no stars, real locations, a plot that could be a footnote.
- *Optional:* [*Umberto D.*](reading-companion.md#umberto-d-1952-de-sica-optional) (1952, De Sica · 89 min)

**Week 12 · Nov 9–15**

- [*L'Avventura*](reading-companion.md#lavventura-1960-antonioni) (1960, Antonioni · 143 min) — a woman disappears and the film loses interest in finding her. Booed at Cannes; then it wasn't.
- [*Il Sorpasso*](reading-companion.md#il-sorpasso-1962-risi) (1962, Risi · 105 min) — the comedy that diagnoses exactly the same postwar emptiness, and is a delight. The pairing is the lesson.
- *Optional:* [*L'Eclisse*](reading-companion.md#leclisse-1962-antonioni-optional) (1962, Antonioni · 126 min)

**Week 13 · Nov 16–22**

- [*8½*](reading-companion.md#8-1963-fellini) (1963, Fellini · 138 min) — a director who can't make his film, making it.
- [*The Conformist*](reading-companion.md#the-conformist-1970-bertolucci) (1970, Bertolucci · 113 min) — Storaro's cinematography is the reason half of New Hollywood looks the way it does. Watch it and then rewatch bits of *The Godfather* in Unit 8.
- *Optional:* [*La Dolce Vita*](reading-companion.md#la-dolce-vita-1960-fellini-optional) (1960, Fellini · 174 min)

**Read:** Criterion's essays on *L'Avventura* and *8½*.

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-4--italy-rubble-to-ennui) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 5 · The French New Wave and What It Stole
### Weeks 14–16 · 23 Nov – 13 Dec 2026

The trick with the New Wave is that it's usually taught as a rupture. It's more useful as theft: a group of critics who loved American genre film and remade it with handheld cameras and no money. Each week here pairs a New Wave film with the thing it was stealing from or handing off to.

**Week 14 · Nov 23–29**

- [*Bob le Flambeur*](reading-companion.md#bob-le-flambeur-1956-melville) (1956, Melville · 98 min) — the low-budget location-shot crime film that showed the younger critics it could be done.
- [*Breathless*](reading-companion.md#breathless-1960-godard) (1960, Godard · 90 min) — then the jump cuts. Having seen Melville first, you'll see what's new and what isn't.
- *Optional:* [*The Killing*](reading-companion.md#the-killing-1956-kubrick-optional) (1956, Kubrick · 84 min)

**Week 15 · Nov 30 – Dec 6**

- [*Cléo from 5 to 7*](reading-companion.md#cléo-from-5-to-7-1962-varda) (1962, Varda · 90 min) — near-real-time, and the best film of the movement by some distance.
- [*Jules and Jim*](reading-companion.md#jules-and-jim-1962-truffaut) (1962, Truffaut · 105 min)
- *Optional:* [*Band of Outsiders*](reading-companion.md#band-of-outsiders-1964-godard-optional) (1964, Godard · 95 min)

**Week 16 · Dec 7–13**

- [*Contempt*](reading-companion.md#contempt-1963-godard) (1963, Godard · 103 min) — Godard with money, widescreen, and Brigitte Bardot; a film about the impossibility of the film it's inside.
- [*Le Samouraï*](reading-companion.md#le-samouraï-1967-melville) (1967, Melville · 105 min) — where the influence loops back. Almost no dialogue; every frame is composition.
- *Optional:* [*Pierrot le Fou*](reading-companion.md#pierrot-le-fou-1965-godard-optional) (1965, Godard · 110 min)

**Read:** Truffaut, "A Certain Tendency of the French Cinema" (1954) — the manifesto, and short.

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-5--the-french-new-wave-and-what-it-stole) in the Reading Companion.

*(Holiday break: Dec 14 – Jan 3. Watch whatever you like, or nothing.)*

## Unit 6 · Japan
### Weeks 17–20 · 4 – 31 Jan 2027

The deepest single national tradition on this list and the biggest single gap for most self-taught viewers. Ozu first, because his style is so rigorously constrained that once you notice the rules, every other director's choices become visible by contrast.

**Week 17 · Jan 4–10 — Ozu**

- [*Late Spring*](reading-companion.md#late-spring-1949-ozu) (1949, Ozu · 108 min)
- [*Tokyo Story*](reading-companion.md#tokyo-story-1953-ozu) (1953, Ozu · 136 min) — the camera sits three feet off the floor and almost never moves. Count how often he cuts to an empty room.
- *Optional:* [*Early Summer*](reading-companion.md#early-summer-1951-ozu-optional) (1951, Ozu · 125 min)

**Week 18 · Jan 11–17 — Kurosawa, at length**

- [*Seven Samurai*](reading-companion.md#seven-samurai-1954-kurosawa) (1954, Kurosawa · 207 min) — take it as the week's whole assignment; split across two nights if you like.
- *Optional:* [*Yojimbo*](reading-companion.md#yojimbo-1961-kurosawa-optional) (1961, Kurosawa · 110 min) — and note that Leone remade it as *A Fistful of Dollars*

**Week 19 · Jan 18–24**

- [*Ugetsu*](reading-companion.md#ugetsu-1953-mizoguchi) (1953, Mizoguchi · 96 min) — long takes, ghost story, and camera movement as moral commentary.
- [*Harakiri*](reading-companion.md#harakiri-1962-kobayashi) (1962, Kobayashi · 133 min) — the samurai film turned into an indictment of the samurai code. Formally immaculate.
- *Optional:* [*Sansho the Bailiff*](reading-companion.md#sansho-the-bailiff-1954-mizoguchi-optional) (1954, Mizoguchi · 124 min)

**Week 20 · Jan 25–31**

- [*High and Low*](reading-companion.md#high-and-low-1963-kurosawa) (1963, Kurosawa · 143 min) — a kidnapping procedural that splits cleanly into two formal halves. The first hour is nearly a filmed play.
- [*Woman in the Dunes*](reading-companion.md#woman-in-the-dunes-1964-teshigahara) (1964, Teshigahara · 147 min) — sand, in extreme close-up, for two and a half hours, and it works.
- *Optional:* [*Tokyo Drifter*](reading-companion.md#tokyo-drifter-1966-suzuki-optional) (1966, Suzuki · 83 min)

**Read:** Bordwell on Ozu (his blog has years of it); Criterion's *Tokyo Story* essay.

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-6--japan) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 7 · The Northern Interior
### Weeks 21–23 · 1 – 21 Feb 2027

The demanding stretch, placed here on purpose — by February you'll have the patience and the vocabulary for it. Bergman, Dreyer, Tarkovsky: films that are about faces, silence, doubt, and duration. Long, slow, and among the highest-value viewing on the list. If any unit is worth the extension film, it's this one.

**Week 21 · Feb 1–7**

- [*Wild Strawberries*](reading-companion.md#wild-strawberries-1957-bergman) (1957, Bergman · 91 min) — the warmest entry point.
- [*The Seventh Seal*](reading-companion.md#the-seventh-seal-1957-bergman) (1957, Bergman · 96 min) — much funnier than its reputation.
- *Optional:* [*Winter Light*](reading-companion.md#winter-light-1963-bergman-optional) (1963, Bergman · 81 min)

**Week 22 · Feb 8–14**

- [*Persona*](reading-companion.md#persona-1966-bergman) (1966, Bergman · 83 min) — the film breaks, literally, in the middle. Two faces merge. Nobody has satisfactorily explained it and that's fine.
- [*Ordet*](reading-companion.md#ordet-1955-dreyer) (1955, Dreyer · 126 min) — the final scene is one of the few in cinema that genuinely cannot be described.
- *Optional:* [*Cries and Whispers*](reading-companion.md#cries-and-whispers-1972-bergman-optional) (1972, Bergman · 91 min)

**Week 23 · Feb 15–21**

- [*Stalker*](reading-companion.md#stalker-1979-tarkovsky) (1979, Tarkovsky · 161 min) — three men walk very slowly toward a room. It is either the most boring thing you've seen or it isn't.
- [*Solaris*](reading-companion.md#solaris-1972-tarkovsky) (1972, Tarkovsky · 167 min) — a long week; drop to one film if you need to and take *Stalker*.
- *Optional:* [*Andrei Rublev*](reading-companion.md#andrei-rublev-1966-tarkovsky-optional) (1966, Tarkovsky · 183 min) — Tarkovsky's preferred cut; the 205-min *Passion According to Andrei* also circulates

**Read:** Tarkovsky, *Sculpting in Time* (excerpts); Criterion essay on *Ordet*.

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-7--the-northern-interior) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 8 · New Hollywood
### Weeks 24–27 · 22 Feb – 21 Mar 2027

Reward for Unit 7, and not a lesser unit for it. American directors who had absorbed exactly the European films you just watched, handed studio money, briefly, before the blockbuster closed the window. You'll now recognize the borrowings — the Antonioni in the drift, the Bertolucci in the light.

**Week 24 · Feb 22–28**

- [*Bonnie and Clyde*](reading-companion.md#bonnie-and-clyde-1967-penn) (1967, Penn · 111 min) — French New Wave technique smuggled into a studio picture. Truffaut and Godard were both approached to direct it.
- [*The Graduate*](reading-companion.md#the-graduate-1967-nichols) (1967, Nichols · 106 min)
- *Optional:* [*The Wild Bunch*](reading-companion.md#the-wild-bunch-1969-peckinpah-optional) (1969, Peckinpah · 145 min)

**Week 25 · Mar 1–7**

- [*The Godfather*](reading-companion.md#the-godfather-1972-coppola) (1972, Coppola · 175 min) — watch Gordon Willis's lighting, having just seen *The Conformist*.
- [*Chinatown*](reading-companion.md#chinatown-1974-polanski) (1974, Polanski · 130 min) — noir rebuilt with everything Unit 2 taught you.
- *Optional:* [*The Godfather Part II*](reading-companion.md#the-godfather-part-ii-1974-coppola-optional) (1974, Coppola · 202 min)

**Week 26 · Mar 8–14**

- [*Nashville*](reading-companion.md#nashville-1975-altman) (1975, Altman · 160 min) — twenty-four characters, overlapping sound, no center. The anti-*Godfather*.
- [*Taxi Driver*](reading-companion.md#taxi-driver-1976-scorsese) (1976, Scorsese · 114 min)
- *Optional:* [*McCabe & Mrs. Miller*](reading-companion.md#mccabe--mrs-miller-1971-altman-optional) (1971, Altman · 120 min)

**Week 27 · Mar 15–21**

- [*Days of Heaven*](reading-companion.md#days-of-heaven-1978-malick) (1978, Malick · 94 min) — shot almost entirely at magic hour. Nestor Almendros, twenty minutes of usable light a day.
- [*Apocalypse Now*](reading-companion.md#apocalypse-now-1979-coppola) (1979, Coppola · 147 min) — the theatrical cut. The window closing.
- *Optional:* [*The Conversation*](reading-companion.md#the-conversation-1974-coppola-optional) (1974, Coppola · 113 min)

**Read:** Pauline Kael on *Nashville*; then Peter Biskind, *Easy Riders, Raging Bulls* (gossipy, fast, and genuinely useful context).

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-8--new-hollywood) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 9 · Hong Kong and Taiwan
### Weeks 28–30 · 22 Mar – 11 Apr 2027

The single most rewarding blind spot on your list. Wong Kar-wai and Edward Yang were making, in the 1990s, some of the best films anyone was making anywhere, and they're barely taught outside film departments.

**Week 28 · Mar 22–28 — Wong Kar-wai**

- [*Days of Being Wild*](reading-companion.md#days-of-being-wild-1990-wong) (1990, Wong · 94 min)
- [*In the Mood for Love*](reading-companion.md#in-the-mood-for-love-2000-wong) (2000, Wong · 98 min) — the film most often named the best of this century. Repetition, blocked sightlines, and a story told almost entirely in what's withheld.
- *Optional:* [*Happy Together*](reading-companion.md#happy-together-1997-wong-optional) (1997, Wong · 96 min)

**Week 29 · Mar 29 – Apr 4**

- [*Yi Yi*](reading-companion.md#yi-yi-2000-yang) (2000, Yang · 173 min) — a family across three generations; possibly the most humane film on this syllabus.
- [*Hard Boiled*](reading-companion.md#hard-boiled-1992-woo) (1992, Woo · 128 min) — the counterweight, and a reminder that this industry also produced the most kinetic action filmmaking on earth.
- *Optional:* [*A City of Sadness*](reading-companion.md#a-city-of-sadness-1989-hou-hsiao-hsien-optional) (1989, Hou Hsiao-hsien · 157 min)

**Week 30 · Apr 5–11**

- [*A Brighter Summer Day*](reading-companion.md#a-brighter-summer-day-1991-yang) (1991, Yang · 237 min) — the week's single assignment. Two nights, and worth every minute.
- *Optional:* [*Chungking Express*](reading-companion.md#chungking-express-1994-wong-kar-wai) rewatch — you'll see a completely different film than you did in Week 2

**Read:** Criterion essays on *In the Mood for Love* and *A Brighter Summer Day*; Bordwell's *Planet Hong Kong* (excerpts).

**After each film:** [essay links for this unit](reading-companion.md#unit-9--hong-kong-and-taiwan) in the Reading Companion.

## Unit 10 · Off the Usual Axis
### Weeks 31–33 · 12 Apr – 2 May 2027

India, Iran, Senegal. Traditions that developed largely outside the Hollywood/Europe conversation, and the correction to a canon that mostly wasn't looking.

**Week 31 · Apr 12–18 — Satyajit Ray**

- [*Pather Panchali*](reading-companion.md#pather-panchali-1955-ray) (1955, Ray · 125 min) — shot on weekends over three years with a mostly amateur crew.
- [*Charulata*](reading-companion.md#charulata-1964-ray) (1964, Ray · 117 min)
- *Optional:* [*Aparajito*](reading-companion.md#aparajito-1956-ray-optional) (1956, Ray · 110 min)

**Week 32 · Apr 19–25 — Iran**

- [*Close-Up*](reading-companion.md#close-up-1990-kiarostami) (1990, Kiarostami · 98 min) — a man impersonates a film director; Kiarostami films the trial and then restages it with the real people. The documentary/fiction boundary simply dissolves. You will have opinions about the epistemology.
- [*Taste of Cherry*](reading-companion.md#taste-of-cherry-1997-kiarostami) (1997, Kiarostami · 99 min)
- *Optional:* [*Where Is the Friend's House?*](reading-companion.md#where-is-the-friends-house-1987-kiarostami-optional) (1987, Kiarostami · 83 min)

**Week 33 · Apr 26 – May 2 — Senegal**

- [*Black Girl*](reading-companion.md#black-girl-1966-sembène) (1966, Sembène · 65 min) — widely considered the first sub-Saharan African feature to reach international audiences.
- [*Touki Bouki*](reading-companion.md#touki-bouki-1973-mambéty) (1973, Mambéty · 88 min) — formally wilder than almost anything in Unit 5.
- *Optional:* [*Xala*](reading-companion.md#xala-1975-sembène-optional) (1975, Sembène · 123 min)

## Unit 11 · Germany and the Political Film
### Weeks 34–36 · 3 – 23 May 2027

Films that treat history as an unresolved argument. The New German Cinema directors were working directly against a national silence, and the political thrillers here were made close enough to their events to be dangerous.

**Week 34 · May 3–9**

- [*Aguirre, the Wrath of God*](reading-companion.md#aguirre-the-wrath-of-god-1972-herzog) (1972, Herzog · 95 min) — shot on a stolen camera in the Peruvian jungle. The production stories are true.
- [*Ali: Fear Eats the Soul*](reading-companion.md#ali-fear-eats-the-soul-1974-fassbinder) (1974, Fassbinder · 93 min) — a remake of Sirk's *All That Heaven Allows*, transposed to Munich and race.

**Week 35 · May 10–16**

- [*The Battle of Algiers*](reading-companion.md#the-battle-of-algiers-1966-pontecorvo) (1966, Pontecorvo · 121 min) — shot documentary-style; the opening title card insists no newsreel footage was used, because viewers assumed otherwise.
- [*Z*](reading-companion.md#z-1969-costa-gavras) (1969, Costa-Gavras · 127 min)
- *Optional:* [*The Marriage of Maria Braun*](reading-companion.md#the-marriage-of-maria-braun-1979-fassbinder-optional) (1979, Fassbinder · 120 min)

**Week 36 · May 17–23**

- [*Wings of Desire*](reading-companion.md#wings-of-desire-1987-wenders) (1987, Wenders · 128 min) — Berlin from above, in monochrome, just before the wall came down.
- [*Night and Fog*](reading-companion.md#night-and-fog-1956-resnais) (1956, Resnais · 32 min) — the most-taught documentary in film curricula, and the one that established how the form handles atrocity: present-tense color tracking shots over the empty camps, cut against black-and-white archive. Watch it before *Come and See*, not after.
- [*Come and See*](reading-companion.md#come-and-see-1985-klimov) (1985, Klimov · 142 min) — the hardest film on this syllabus by a wide margin, and unarguably a great one. ## Unit 13 · Capstone — Cinema as Pure Form
### Weeks 40–42 · 14 Jun – 4 Jul 2027

Three weeks of films that are difficult only without context. You now have the context. This unit would have been miserable in September and should be exhilarating in June — which is itself the demonstration that the whole approach worked.

**Week 40 · Jun 14–20**

- [*2001: A Space Odyssey*](reading-companion.md#2001-a-space-odyssey-1968-kubrick) (1968, Kubrick · 149 min)

- [*La Jetée*](reading-companion.md#la-jetée-1962-marker) (1962, Marker · 28 min) — told almost entirely in still photographs: a film about time and memory that questions whether cinema needs motion at all. Sitting beside Kubrick's vision of the future, it's the better companion piece of the two.

## Slack built in

Three weeks are single-film weeks by design (18, 30, 42). The holiday break is three weeks of nothing. Extensions are always optional. If you finish early, the extension list alone is another 40 films.

The four weeks carrying an extra title (9, 10, 36, 40) add 104 minutes between them across the whole year — roughly one additional feature, spread over ten months. They exist to close two gaps that every academic film course treats as essential and this plan originally skipped entirely: **German Expressionism**, and **non-narrative film** (avant-garde and documentary). Four short films is a cheap price for both.
